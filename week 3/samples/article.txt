Remote work has changed how companies hire, collaborate and measure success. Before 2020, most knowledge workers commuted to a central office, and managers often equated presence with productivity. When lockdowns forced teams online, many firms discovered that output did not fall as feared, and some employees reported higher focus without office interruptions.

However, remote work also created new challenges. Junior employees struggled to learn informally from colleagues, spontaneous idea-sharing declined, and some workers felt isolated. Companies responded with hybrid schedules, written documentation habits and regular in-person gatherings.

Today, the debate has shifted from "remote versus office" to "how to design work deliberately". Organisations that set clear goals, communicate in writing and invest in onboarding tend to report better results than those that simply copy their old office routines onto video calls.
