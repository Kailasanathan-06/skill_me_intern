# Introduction to Renewable Energy

Renewable energy comes from sources that replenish naturally, such as sunlight, wind, water and geothermal heat. Unlike coal, oil and gas, these sources do not run out and they produce little or no greenhouse gas while generating electricity. Many countries now treat renewables as central to their climate and energy-security plans.

# Solar and Wind Power

Solar panels convert sunlight directly into electricity using photovoltaic cells, and their cost has fallen sharply over the last decade. Wind turbines turn the kinetic energy of moving air into power and work best on coasts, plains and offshore sites. Both technologies are intermittent, meaning output rises and falls with the weather and time of day.

# Storage and the Grid

Because solar and wind are intermittent, storage is essential. Lithium-ion batteries smooth short-term swings, while pumped hydro stores large amounts of energy for hours or days. Modern grids also use smart meters and demand response so that consumption can shift to times when clean power is plentiful.

# Challenges and Outlook

Key obstacles include upfront investment, grid upgrades, permitting delays and the mining of minerals needed for batteries. Even so, analysts expect renewables to supply a growing share of global electricity, driven by falling costs and supportive policy. Workforce training and recycling programmes will matter as the sector expands.
